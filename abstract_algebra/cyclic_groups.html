Cyclic Groups

Definition of cyclic groups

\(\forall\) group \(\langle G, *\rangle\) is called a cyclic group iff \(\exists\ g \in G\), \(\forall\ a \in G\), \(\exists\ n \in \mathbb{Z}\), \(a = g^{n}\).
\(g\) generates \(\langle G, *\rangle\) and is called a generator of \(\langle G, *\rangle\). \(\langle G, *\rangle\) is denoted as \(\langle g, *\rangle\).

Cyclic groups are abelian

\(\forall\) cyclic group \(\langle G, *\rangle\) is abelian.
Proof. Let \(g \in G\) be a generator of \(\langle G, *\rangle\). \(\forall\ a, b \in G\), \(\exists\ m, n \in \mathbb{Z}^{+}\), \(a = g^{m}\) and \(b = g^{n}\). Then \[ a * b = g^{m} * g^{n} = g^{m + n} = g^{n + m} = g^{n} * g^{m} = b * a. \] Hence, \(*\) is communicative. \(\forall\) cyclic group \(\langle G, *\rangle\) is abelian.

\(\blacksquare\)

Isomorphism of cyclic groups

  • \(\forall\) infinite cyclic group \(\langle G, *\rangle\) is isomorphic to \(\langle \mathbb{Z}, +\rangle\).
  • \(\forall\ n \in \mathbb{Z}^{+}\), \(\forall\) finite cyclic group \(\langle G, *\rangle\) with order \(n\) is isomorphic to \(\langle \mathbb{Z}_{n}, +_{n}\rangle\).
Proof.
  • \(\forall\) infinite cyclic group \(\langle G, *\rangle\), \(\exists\ g \in G\) is a generator of \(\langle G, *\rangle\). Let \(f: G \rightarrow \mathbb{Z}\) defined as \(\forall\ a \in G\), \(\exists\ n \in \mathbb{Z}\), \(a = g^{n}\), there is \(f(a) = n\).
    • This proof proves \(f\) is a bijective function.
      • \(f\) is an injective function.
        \(\forall\ a_{1}, a_{2} \in G\), \(\exists\ n_{1}, n_{2} \in \mathbb{Z}\), \(a_{1} = g^{n_{1}}\) and \(a_{2} = g^{n_{2}}\). If \(a_{1} \ne a_{2}\), then \(g^{n_{1}} \ne g^{n_{2}}\), which means that \(n_{1} \ne n_{2}\). Then, \(f(a_{1}) = n_{1} \ne n_{2} = f(a_{2})\). Therefore, \(f\) is an injective function.
      • \(f\) is a surjective function.
        \(\forall\ n \in \mathbb{Z}\), \(\exists\ a \in G\), \(a = g^{n}\). Then, \(f(a) = n\). Therefore, \(f\) is a surjective function.
      Because \(f\) is both injective and surjective, \(f\) is a bijective function.
    • This proof proves \(\langle G, *\rangle \simeq \langle \mathbb{Z}, +\rangle\).
      \(\forall\ a_{1}, a_{2} \in G\), \(\exists\ n_{1}, n_{2} \in \mathbb{Z}\), \(a_{1} = g^{n_{1}}\) and \(a_{2} = g^{n_{2}}\). There is \[ f(a_{1} * a_{2}) = f(g^{n_{1}} * g^{n_{2}}) = f(g^{n_{1} + n_{2}}) = n_{1} + n_{2} = f(g^{n_{1}}) + f(g^{n_{2}}) = f(a_{1}) + f(a_{2}). \]
    To sum up, \(\forall\) infinite cyclic group \(\langle G, *\rangle\) is isomorphic to \(\langle \mathbb{Z}, +\rangle\).
  • \(\forall\ n \in \mathbb{Z}^{+}\), \(\forall\) finite cyclic group \(\langle G, *\rangle\) with order \(n\), \(\exists\ g \in G\) is a generator of \(\langle G, *\rangle\). Let \(f: G \rightarrow \mathbb{Z}_{n}\) defined as \(\forall\ a \in G\), \(\exists\ k \in \mathbb{Z}_{n}\), \(a = g^{k}\), there is \(f(a) = k\).
    • This proof proves \(f\) is a bijective function.
      • \(f\) is an injective function.
        \(\forall\ a_{1}, a_{2} \in G\), \(\exists\ n_{1}, n_{2} \in \mathbb{Z}\), \(a_{1} = g^{n_{1}}\) and \(a_{2} = g^{n_{2}}\). If \(a_{1} \ne a_{2}\), then \(g^{n_{1}} \ne g^{n_{2}}\), which means that \(n_{1} \ne n_{2}\). Then, \(f(a_{1}) = n_{1} \ne n_{2} = f(a_{2})\). Therefore, \(f\) is an injective function.
      • \(f\) is a surjective function.
        \(\forall\ n \in Z\), \(\exists\ a \in G\), \(a = g^{n}\). Then, \(f(a) = n\). Therefore, \(f\) is a surjective function.
      Because \(f\) is both injective and surjective, \(f\) is a bijective function.
    • This proof proves \(\langle G, *\rangle \simeq \langle \mathbb{Z}, +\rangle\).
      \(\forall\ a_{1}, a_{2} \in G\), \(\exists\ n_{1}, n_{2} \in \mathbb{Z}\), \(a_{1} = g^{n_{1}}\) and \(a_{2} = g^{n_{2}}\). There is \[ f(a_{1} * a_{2}) = f(g^{n_{1}} * g^{n_{2}}) = f(g^{n_{1} + n_{2}}) = n_{1} + n_{2} = f(g^{n_{1}}) + f(g^{n_{2}}) = f(a_{1}) + f(a_{2}). \]
    To sum up, \(\forall\) infinite cyclic group \(\langle G, *\rangle\) is isomorphic to \(\langle \mathbb{Z}, +\rangle\).

\(\blacksquare\)
