n-fold Cartesian Product of Field

Definition

\(F^{n}\) is the set of all tuples of length \(n\) of elements of \(F\). \[ F^{n} = \{(x_{1}, \dots, x_{n})\ |\ x_{1}, \dots, x_{n} \in F\ \text{and}\ n \in \mathbb{Z}^{+}\}. \] \(\forall\ i \in \{1, \dots, n\}\), \(x_{i}\) is called the \(i^{\text{th}}\) coordinate of \((x_{1}, \dots, x_{n})\)

Equality

\(\forall\ (x_{1}, \dots, x_{n}), (y_{1}, \dots, y_{n}) \in F^{n}\), \((x_{1}, \dots, x_{n}) = (y_{1}, \dots, y_{n})\) iff \(\forall\ i \in \{1, \dots, n\}\), \(x_{i} = y_{i}\).

Operations

Addition \(+:\ F^{n} \times F^{n} \rightarrow F^{n}\)

\(\forall\ (x_{1}, \dots, x_{n}), (y_{1}, \dots, y_{n}) \in F^{n}\), \[ (x_{1}, \dots, x_{n}) + (y_{1}, \dots, y_{n}) = (x_{1} + y_{1}, \dots, x_{n} + y_{n}). \]

Scalar multiplication \(\cdot:\ F \times F^{n} \rightarrow F^{n}\)

\(\forall\ \lambda \in F\) and \(\forall\ (x_{1}, \dots, x_{n}) \in F^{n}\), \[ \lambda (x_{1}, \dots, x_{n}) = (\lambda x_{1}, \dots, \lambda x_{n}). \]

Proof.
  • Communtativity:
    \(\forall\ (x_{1}, \dots, x_{n}), (y_{1}, \dots, y_{n}) \in F^{n}\), \[ \begin{align*} (x_{1}, \dots, x_{n}) + (y_{1}, \dots, y_{n}) &= (x_{1} + y_{1}, \dots, x_{n} + y_{n}) \\ &= (y_{1} + x_{1}, \dots, y_{n} + x_{n}) \\ &= (y_{1}, \dots, y_{n}) + (x_{1}, \dots, x_{n}). \end{align*} \]
  • Associativity:
    \(\forall\ (x_{1}, \dots, x_{n}), (y_{1}, \dots, y_{n}), (z_{1}, \dots, z_{n}) \in F^{n}\), \[ \begin{align*} [(x_{1}, \dots, x_{n}) + (y_{1}, \dots, y_{n})] + (z_{1}, \dots, z_{n}) &= (x_{1} + y_{1}, \dots, x_{n} + y_{n}) + (z_{1}, \dots, z_{n})\\ &= ((x_{1} + y_{1}) + z_{1}, \dots, (x_{n} + y_{n}) + z_{n}) \\ &= (x_{1} + (y_{1} + z_{1}), \dots, x_{n} + (y_{n} + z_{n})) \\ &= (x_{1}, \dots, x_{n}) + (y_{1} + z_{1}, \dots, y_{n} + z_{n}) \\ &= (x_{1}, \dots, x_{n}) + [(y_{1}, \dots, y_{n})] + (z_{1}, \dots, z_{n})]. \end{align*} \] \(\forall\ \lambda, \mu \in F\) and \(\forall\ (x_{1}, \dots, x_{n}) \in F^{n}\), \[ \begin{align*} (\lambda\mu)\cdot(x_{1}, \dots, x_{n}) &= ((\lambda\mu)x_{1}, \dots, (\lambda\mu)x_{n}) \\ &= (\lambda(\mu x_{1}), \dots, \lambda(\mu x_{n})) \\ &= \lambda(\mu x_{1}, \dots, \mu x_{n}) \\ &= \lambda[\mu(x_{1}, \dots, x_{n})]. \end{align*} \]
  • Additive identity:
    \((0, \dots, 0) \in F^{n}\). \(\forall\ (x_{1}, \dots, x_{n}) \in F^{n}\), \[ (x_{1}, \dots, x_{n}) + (0, \dots, 0) = (x_{1} + 0, \dots, x_{n} + 0) = (x_{1}, \dots, x_{n}). \] Therefore, \(\textbf{0} = (0, \dots, 0)\).
  • Additive inverse:
    \(\forall\ (x_{1}, \dots, x_{n}) \in F^{n}\), \((-x_{1}, \dots, -x_{n}) \in F^{n}\). \[ (x_{1}, \dots, x_{n}) + (-x_{1}, \dots, -x_{n}) = (x_{1} + (-x_{1})), \dots, x_{n} + (-x_{n})) = (0, \dots, 0) = \textbf{0}. \] Therefore, \((-x_{1}, \dots, -x_{n})\) is the additive inverse of \((x_{1}, \dots, x_{n})\).
  • Multiplicative identity:
    \(\forall\ (x_{1}, \dots, x_{n}) \in F^{n}\), \[ 1 \cdot (x_{1}, \dots, x_{n}) = (1 \cdot x_{1}, \dots, 1 \cdot x_{n}) = (x_{1}, \dots, x_{n}). \]
  • Distributive properties:
    \(\forall\ \lambda \in F\) and \(\forall\ (x_{1}, \dots, x_{n}), (y_{1}, \dots, y_{n}) \in F^{n}\), \[ \begin{align*} \lambda[(x_{1}, \dots, x_{n}) + (y_{1}, \dots, y_{n})] &= \lambda(x_{1} + y_{1}, \dots, x_{n} + y_{n}) \\ &= (\lambda(x_{1} + y_{1}), \dots, \lambda(x_{n} + y_{n})) \\ &= (\lambda x_{1} + \lambda y_{1}, \dots, \lambda x_{n} + \lambda y_{n}) \\ &= (\lambda x_{1}, \dots, \lambda x_{n}) + (\lambda y_{1}, \dots, \lambda y_{n}) \\ &= \lambda(x_{1}, \dots, x_{n}) + \lambda(y_{1}, \dots, y_{n}). \end{align*} \] \(\forall\ \lambda, \mu \in F\) and \(\forall\ (x_{1}, \dots, x_{n}) \in F^{n}\), \[ \begin{align*} (\lambda + \mu)(x_{1}, \dots, x_{n}) &= ((\lambda + \mu)x_{1}, \dots, (\lambda + \mu)x_{n}) \\ &= (\lambda x_{1} + \mu x_{1}, \dots, \lambda x_{n} + \mu x_{n}) \\ &= (\lambda x_{1}, \dots, \lambda x_{n}) + (\mu x_{1}, \dots, \mu x_{n}) \\ &= \lambda(x_{1}, \dots, x_{n}) + \mu(x_{1}, \dots, x_{n}). \end{align*} \]

\(\blacksquare\)

TBD: Basis and dimension of \(F^{n}\)

\(\forall\ i \in \{1, \cdots, n\}\), let \(\textbf{v}_{i}\) be a vector in \(F^{n}\) such that all coordinates in \(\textbf{v}_{i}\) are \(0\) except the \(i^{\text{th}}\) coordinate is \(1\). \(\{\textbf{v}_{1}, \dots, \textbf{v}_{n}\}\) is a basis of \(F^{n}\). \(\text{dim}F^{n} = n\).
Proof.
  • \(\{\textbf{v}_{1}, \dots, \textbf{v}_{n}\}\) is linearly independent.
    \(\forall\ a_{1}, \dots, a_{n} \in F\), \[ a_{1}\textbf{v}_{1} + \cdots + a_{n}\textbf{v}_{n} = a_{1}(1, \dots, 0) + \cdots + a_{n}(0, \dots, 1) = (a_{1}, \dots, 0) + \cdots + (0, \dots, a_{n}) = (a_{1}, \dots, a_{n}) \] If \(a_{1}\textbf{v}_{1} + \cdots + a_{n}\textbf{v}_{n} = \textbf{0}\), then there is \((a_{1}, \dots, a_{n}) = (0, \dots, 0)\). Therefore, \(a_{1} = \cdots = a_{n} = 0\). Hence, \(\{\textbf{v}_{1}, \dots, \textbf{v}_{n}\}\) is linearly independent.
  • \(\text{span}(\{\textbf{v}_{1}, \dots, \textbf{v}_{n}\}) = F^{n}\).
    \(\forall\ (a_{1}, \dots, a_{n}) \in F^{n}\), \[ (a_{1}, \dots, a_{n}) = (a_{1}, \dots, 0) + \cdots + (0, \dots, a_{n}) = a_{1}(1, \dots, 0) + \cdots + a_{n}(0, \dots, 1) = a_{1}\textbf{v}_{1} + \cdots + a_{n}\textbf{v}_{n}. \] Hence, \(\text{span}(\{\textbf{v}_{1}, \dots, \textbf{v}_{n}\}) = F^{n}\).
To sum up, \(\{\textbf{v}_{1}, \dots, \textbf{v}_{n}\}\) is a basis of \(F^{n}\). \(\text{dim}F^{n} = n\).

\(\blacksquare\)
