Relations

Definition of binary relations

  1. \(\forall\) sets \(A\) and \(B\), a binary relation \(R\) between \(A\) and \(B\) is a subset of \(A \times B\). \(\forall\ a \in A\) and \(\forall\ b \in B\), \((a, b) \in R\) is denoted as \(a R b\).
  2. \(\forall\) set \(S\), a binary relation \(R\) on \(S\) is a subset of \(S \times S\).

Definition of equivalence relations

\(\forall\) set \(S\), a binary relation \(R\) on \(S\) is an equivalence relation on \(S\) iff
  • Reflexive:
    \(\forall\ a \in S\), \(aRa\).
  • Symmetric:
    \(\forall\ a, b \in S\), if \(aRb\), then \(bRa\).
  • Transitive:
    \(\forall\ a, b, c \in S\), if \(aRb\) and \(bRc\), then \(aRc\).
An equivalence relation is denoted as \(\sim\).
\(\forall\ a \in S\), the equivalence class of \(a\) on \(\sim\) is a set \(E_{\sim}(a)\) defined as \(\{x\ |\ x \sim a, x \in S\}\). \(\forall\ x \in E_{\sim}(a)\) is equivalent to \(a\) and \(x\) is called a representative of \(E_{\sim}(a)\).

Equivalence class in equivalence relation

\(\forall\) set \(S\), \(\forall\) equivalence relation \(\sim\) on \(S\), \(\forall\ a, b \in S\), if \(a \sim b\), then \(E_{\sim}(a) = E_{\sim}(b)\).
Proof.
  • The following proves that \(E_{\sim}(a) \subseteq E_{\sim}(b)\).
    \(\forall\ x \in E_{\sim}(a)\), \(x \sim a\). Because \(a \sim b\), \(x \sim b\), \(x \in E_{\sim}(b)\).
  • The following proves that \(E_{\sim}(b) \subseteq E_{\sim}(a)\).
    \(\forall\ x \in E_{\sim}(b)\), \(x \sim b\). Because \(a \sim b\), \(b \sim a\), \(x \sim a\), \(x \in E_{\sim}(a)\).
Hence, \(E_{\sim}(a) = E_{\sim}(b)\).

\(\blacksquare\)

Equivalence relation and partition

\(\forall\) nonempty set \(S\),
  • \(\forall\) equivalence relation \(\sim\) on \(S\), \(\{E_{\sim}(x)\ |\ x \in S\}\) is a partition of \(S\).
  • \(\forall\) partition \(\{S_{i}\ |\ i \in I\}\) of \(S\), where \(I\) is an index set, \(\exists\) equivalence relation \(\sim\) on \(S\), \(\{E_{\sim}(x)\ |\ x \in S\} = \{S_{i}\ |\ i \in I\}\).
Proof.
    • \(\forall\ E \in \{E_{\sim}(x)\ |\ x \in S\}\), \(\exists\ a \in S\), \(E = E_{\sim}(a)\). Because \(\sim\) is an equivalence relation, \(a \sim a\). Therefore, \(a \in E\), \(E \ne \varnothing\).
    • \(\forall\ a \in S\), because \(\sim\) is an equivalence relation, \(a \sim a\). Then, \(a \in E_{\sim}(a)\). Because \(E_{\sim}(a) \in \{E_{\sim}(x)\ |\ x \in S\}\), \(E_{\sim}(a) \subseteq \bigcup_{x \in S}E_{\sim}(x)\). Therefore, \(a \in \bigcup_{x \in S}E_{\sim}(x)\).
    • \(\forall\ E_{1}, E_{2} \in \{E_{\sim}(x)\ |\ x \in S\}\), \(\exists\ x_{1}, x_{2} \in S\), \(E_{1} = E_{\sim}(x_{1})\) and \(E_{2} = E_{\sim}(x_{2})\). If \(E_{1} \ne E_{2}\), then \(\exists\ y \in E_{2}\), \((y, x_{1}) \notin \sim\). Suppose that \(E_{1} \cap E_{2} \ne \varnothing\), \(\exists\ z \in E_{1} \cap E_{2}\).
      • Because \(z \in E_{1}\), \(z \sim x_{1}\), \(x_{1} \sim z\). \(\forall\ y_{1} \in E_{1}\), \(y_{1} \sim x_{1}\), \(y_{1} \sim z\).
      • Because \(z \in E_{2}\), \(z \sim x_{2}\), \(x_{2} \sim z\). \(\forall\ y_{2} \in E_{2}\), \(y_{2} \sim x_{1}\), \(y_{2} \sim z\), \(z \sim y_{2}\).
      Because \(y_{1} \sim z\) and \(z \sim y_{2}\), \(y_{1} \sim y_{2}\). Because \(x_{1} \in E_{1}\) and \(y \in E_{2}\), \(x_{1} \sim y\), \(y \sim x_{1}\), which contradicts \((y, x_{1}) \notin \sim\). Therefore, \(E_{1} \cap E_{2} = \varnothing\).
    To sum up, \(\{E_{\sim}(x)\ |\ x \in S\}\) is a partition of \(S\).
  • Define a binary relation \(R\) on \(S\) as the following way: \(\forall\ x, y \in S\), because \(\{S_{i}\ |\ i \in I\}\) is a partition of \(S\), \(\exists\ i, j \in I\), \(x \in S_{i}\) and \(y \in S_{j}\). Then \(xRy\) iff \(i = j\).
    1. The following proves that the relation is well-defined.
      \(\forall\ x, y \in S\), because \(\{S_{i}\ |\ i \in I\}\) is a partition of \(S\), \(\exists !\ i, j \in I\), \(x \in S_{i}\) and \(y \in S_{j}\). Therefore, either \(i = j\) or \(i \ne j\).
    2. The following proves that \(R\) is an equivalence relation \(\sim\) on \(S\).
      • Reflexive:
        \(\forall\ x \in S\), because \(\{S_{i}\ |\ i \in I\}\) is a partition of \(S\), \(\exists !\ i \in I\), \(x \in S_{i}\). Because \(i = i\), there is \(xRx\).
      • Symmetric:
        \(\forall\ x, y \in S\), because \(\{S_{i}\ |\ i \in I\}\) is a partition of \(S\), \(\exists !\ i, j \in I\), \(x \in S_{i}\) and \(y \in S_{j}\). If \(xRy\), then \(i = j\). Then \(j = i\), there is \(yRx\).
      • Transitive:
        \(\forall\ x, y, z \in S\), because \(\{S_{i}\ |\ i \in I\}\) is a partition of \(S\), \(\exists !\ i, j, k \in I\), \(x \in S_{i}\), \(y \in S_{j}\) and \(z \in S_{k}\). If \(xRy\) and \(yRz\), then \(i = j\) and \(j = k\). Therefore, \(i = k\), there is \(xRz\).
      Therefore, \(R\) is an equivalence relation \(\sim\) on \(S\).
    3. The following proves that \(\{E_{\sim}(x)\ |\ x \in S\} = \{S_{i}\ |\ i \in I\}\).
      • The following proves that \(\{E_{\sim}(x)\ |\ x \in S\} \subseteq \{S_{i}\ |\ i \in I\}\).
        \(\forall\ E \in \{E_{\sim}(x)\ |\ x \in S\}\), \(\exists\ x \in S\), \(E = E_{\sim}(x)\). Because \(\{S_{i}\ |\ i \in I\}\) is a partition of \(S\), \(\exists !\ i \in I\), \(x \in S_{i}\). The following proves that \(E_{\sim}(x) = S_{i}\).
        • The following proves that \(E_{\sim}(x) \subseteq S_{i}\).
          \(\forall\ y \in E_{\sim}(x)\), \(y \in S\) and \(y \sim x\). Because \(\{S_{i}\ |\ i \in I\}\) is a partition of \(S\), \(\exists !\ j \in I\), \(y \in S_{j}\). Because \(y \sim x\), \(j = i\). Therefore, \(y \in S_{i}\), \(E_{\sim}(x) \subseteq S_{i}\).
        • The following proves that \(S_{i} \subseteq E_{\sim}(x)\).
          \(\forall\ y \in S_{i}\), because \(i = i\), \(y \sim x\). Therefore, \(y \in E_{\sim}(x)\), \(S_{i} \subseteq E_{\sim}(x)\).
        To sum up, \(E_{\sim}(x) = S_{i}\). Because \(E = E_{\sim}(x)\), \(E = S_{i}\). Because \(S_{i} \in \{S_{i}\ |\ i \in I\}\), \(E \in \{S_{i}\ |\ i \in I\}\), \(\{E_{\sim}(x)\ |\ x \in S\} \subseteq \{S_{i}\ |\ i \in I\}\).
      • The following proves that \(\{S_{i}\ |\ i \in I\} \subseteq \{E_{\sim}(x)\ |\ x \in S\}\).
        \(\forall\ S_{i} \in \{S_{i}\ |\ i \in I\}\), where \(i \in I\), \(\exists\ x \in S_{i}\). Because \(S_{i} \subseteq S\), \(x \in S\), \(E_{\sim}(x) = \{y\ |\ y \sim x, y \in S\}\). The following proves that \(S_{i} = E_{\sim}(x)\).
        • The following proves that \(S_{i} \subseteq E_{\sim}(x)\).
          \(\forall\ y \in S_{i}\), because \(i = i\), \(y \sim x\). Therefore, \(y \in E_{\sim}(x)\), \(S_{i} \subseteq E_{\sim}(x)\).
        • The following proves that \(E_{\sim}(x) \subseteq S_{i}\).
          \(\forall\ y \in E_{\sim}(x)\), \(y \in S\) and \(y \sim x\). Because \(\{S_{i}\ |\ i \in I\}\) is a partition of \(S\), \(\exists !\ j \in I\), \(y \in S_{j}\). Because \(y \sim x\), \(j = i\). Therefore, \(y \in S_{i}\), \(E_{\sim}(x) \subseteq S_{i}\).
        To sum up, \(S_{i} = E_{\sim}(x)\). Because \(E_{\sim}(x) \in \{E_{\sim}(x)\ |\ x \in S\}\), \(S_{i} \in \{E_{\sim}(x)\ |\ x \in S\}\), \(\{S_{i}\ |\ i \in I\} \subseteq \{E_{\sim}(x)\ |\ x \in S\}\).
      To sum up, \(\{E_{\sim}(x)\ |\ x \in S\} = \{S_{i}\ |\ i \in I\}\).

\(\blacksquare\)
