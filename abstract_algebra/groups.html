Groups

Definition of groups

\(\forall\) binary algebraic structure \(\langle G, *\rangle\) is a group iff
  • \(*\) is associative.
  • \(\langle G, *\rangle\) has the identity element.
  • \(\langle G, *\rangle\) has inverses.
The order of \(\langle G, *\rangle\) is the number of elements in \(G\), which is denoted as \(|G|\).
\(\forall\) group \(\langle G, *\rangle\) is abelian iff \(*\) is commutative.

Equivalent definitions of groups

\(\forall\) binary algebraic structure \(\langle G, *\rangle\) such that \(*\) is associative and
  1. \(\langle G, *\rangle\) has the identity element and \(\langle G, *\rangle\) has inverses.
  2. \(\exists\ e \in G\) is a left identity element of \(\langle G, *\rangle\) and \(\langle G, *\rangle\) has left inverses based on \(e\).
  3. \(\exists\ e \in G\) is a right identity element of \(\langle G, *\rangle\) and \(\langle G, *\rangle\) has right inverses based on \(e\).
  4. \(\exists\ e \in G\) is a left identity element of \(\langle G, *\rangle\) and \(\langle G, *\rangle\) has right inverses based on \(e\).
  5. \(\exists\ e \in G\) is a right identity element of \(\langle G, *\rangle\) and \(\langle G, *\rangle\) has left inverses based on \(e\).
There are
  • Condition 1 \(\Leftrightarrow\) Condition 2.
  • Condition 1 \(\Leftrightarrow\) Condition 3.
  • Condition 1 \(\Rightarrow\) Condition 4. Condition 4 \(\nRightarrow\) Condition 1.
  • Condition 1 \(\Rightarrow\) Condition 5. Condition 5 \(\nRightarrow\) Condition 1.
Proof.
  • Condition 1 \(\Rightarrow\) Condition 2, Condition 3, Condition 4, Condition 5.
    Let \(e \in G\) be the identity element of \(\langle G, *\rangle\). \(\forall\ a \in G\), \(e * a = a * e = a\). Therefore, \(e\) is both a left identity element and a right identity element of \(\langle G, *\rangle\). \(\forall\ a \in G\), \(\exists\ b \in G\), \(b * a = a * b = e\). Therefore, \(\langle G, *\rangle\) has both left inverses based on \(e\) and right inverses based on \(e\).
  • Condition 2 \(\Rightarrow\) Condition 1.
    • The proof that \(\langle G, *\rangle\) has the identity element.
      \(\forall\ a \in G\), \(\exists\ b \in G\) such that \(b * a = e\). \(\exists\ c \in G\) such that \(c * b = e\). There are \[ c * (b * a) = c * e, \] and \[ c * (b * a) = (c * b) * a = e * a = a. \] Therefore, \(c * e = a\). Moreover, there are \[ (c * e) * e = a * e, \] and \[ (c * e) * e = c * (e * e) = c * e = a. \] Therefore, \(a * e = a\), and \(e\) is also a right identity element of \(\langle G, *\rangle\). Hence, \(e\) is the identity element of \(\langle G, *\rangle\).
    • The proof that \(\langle G, *\rangle\) has inverses.
      Because \(e\) is the identity element of \(\langle G, *\rangle\), there is \(c * e = c\). Because \(c * e = a\), there is \(c = a\). Because \(c * b = e\), there is \(a * b = e\). Therefore, \(b * a = a * b = e\). Hence, \(b\) is the inverse of \(a\) and \(\langle G, *\rangle\) has inverses.
  • Condition 3 \(\Rightarrow\) Condition 1.
    • The proof that \(\langle G, *\rangle\) has the identity element.
      \(\forall\ a \in G\), \(\exists\ b \in G\) such that \(a * b = e\). \(\exists\ c \in G\) such that \(b * c = e\). There are \[ (a * b) * c = e * c, \] and \[ (a * b) * c = a * (b * c) = a * e = a. \] Therefore, \(e * c = a\). Moreover, there are \[ e * (e * c) = e * a, \] and \[ e * (e * c) = (e * e) * c = e * c = a. \] Therefore, \(e * a = a\), and \(e\) is also a left identity element of \(\langle G, *\rangle\). Hence, \(e\) is the identity element of \(\langle G, *\rangle\).
    • The proof that \(\langle G, *\rangle\) has inverses.
      Because \(e\) is the identity element of \(\langle G, *\rangle\), there is \(e * c = c\). Because \(e * c = a\), there is \(c = a\). Because \(b * c = e\), there is \(b * a = e\). Therefore, \(b * a = a * b = e\). Hence, \(b\) is the inverse of \(a\) and \(\langle G, *\rangle\) has inverses.
  • Condition 4 \(\nRightarrow\) Condition 1.
    Let \(G = \{a, e\}\) and the operation \(*\) be
    \(*\) \(a\) \(e\)
    \(a\) \(a\) \(e\)
    \(e\) \(a\) \(e\)
    There are \[ (a * a) * a = a * a = a, \qquad a * (a * a) = a * a = a. \] \[ (a * a) * e = a * e = e, \qquad a * (a * e) = a * e = e. \] \[ (a * e) * a = e * a = a, \qquad a * (e * a) = a * a = a. \] \[ (e * a) * a = a * a = a, \qquad e * (a * a) = e * a = a. \] \[ (a * e) * e = e * e = e, \qquad a * (e * e) = a * e = e. \] \[ (e * a) * e = a * e = e, \qquad e * (a * e) = e * e = e. \] \[ (e * e) * a = e * a = a, \qquad e * (e * a) = e * a = a. \] \[ (e * e) * e = e * e = e, \qquad e * (e * e) = e * e = e. \] Therefore, \(*\) is associative. Because \(e * a = a\) and \(e * e = e\), \(e\) is a left identity of \(\langle G, *\rangle\). Because \(a * e = e\), \(e\) is a right inverse of \(a\). Because \(e * e = e\), \(e\) is a right inverse of \(e\). Therefore, \(\langle G, *\rangle\) has right inverses based on \(e\). However, \(e * a = a \ne e = a * e\), \(e\) is not a right identity of \(\langle G, *\rangle\). \(\langle G, *\rangle\) is not a group.
  • Condition 5 \(\nRightarrow\) Condition 1.
    Let \(G = \{a, e\}\) and the operation \(*\) be
    \(*\) \(a\) \(e\)
    \(a\) \(a\) \(a\)
    \(e\) \(e\) \(e\)
    There are \[ (a * a) * a = a * a = a, \qquad a * (a * a) = a * a = a. \] \[ (a * a) * e = a * e = a, \qquad a * (a * e) = a * a = a. \] \[ (a * e) * a = a * a = a, \qquad a * (e * a) = a * e = a. \] \[ (e * a) * a = e * a = e, \qquad e * (a * a) = e * a = e. \] \[ (a * e) * e = a * e = a, \qquad a * (e * e) = a * e = a. \] \[ (e * a) * e = e * e = e, \qquad e * (a * e) = e * a = e. \] \[ (e * e) * a = e * a = e, \qquad e * (e * a) = e * e = e. \] \[ (e * e) * e = e * e = e, \qquad e * (e * e) = e * e = e. \] Therefore, \(*\) is associative. Because \(a * e = a\) and \(e * e = e\), \(e\) is a right identity of \(\langle G, *\rangle\). Because \(e * a = e\), \(e\) is a left inverse of \(a\). Because \(e * e = e\), \(e\) is a left inverse of \(e\). Therefore, \(\langle G, *\rangle\) has left inverses based on \(e\). However, \(e * a = e \ne a = a * e\), \(e\) is not a left identity of \(\langle G, *\rangle\). \(\langle G, *\rangle\) is not a group.

\(\blacksquare\)

Unique solutions and inverses

  • Unique solutions
    \(\forall\) group \(\langle G, *\rangle\), \(\forall\ a, b \in G\), \(\exists !\ x, y \in G\) such that \(a * x = b\) and \(y * a = b\).
  • Unique inverses
    \(\forall\) group \(\langle G, *\rangle\), \(\forall\ a \in G\), \(\exists !\ b \in G\) is the inverse of \(a\). \(b\) is denoted as \(a^{-1}\).
Proof. Let \(e \in G\) be the identity element of \(\langle G, *\rangle\).
  • Unique solutions
    • Existence
      \(\forall\ a \in G\), \(\exists\ c \in G\) is an inverse of \(a\).
      • Let \(x = c * b \in G\). There is \(a * x = a * (c * b) = (a * c) * b = e * b = b\).
      • Let \(y = b * c \in G\). There is \(y * a = (b * c) * a = b * (c * a) = b * e = b\).
      Hence, \(\exists\ x = c * b \in G\) such that \(a * x = b\). \(\exists\ y = b * c \in G\) such that \(y * a = b\).
    • Uniqueness
      • Suppose \(\exists\ x_{1}, x_{2} \in G\) such that \(a * x_{1} = b\) and \(a * x_{2} = b\) and \(x_{1} \ne x_{2}\). \(\exists\ c \in G\) is an inverse of \(a\), there are \[ c * b = c * (a * x_{1}) = (c * a) * x_{1} = e * x_{1} = x_{1}, \] and \[ c * b = c * (a * x_{2}) = (c * a) * x_{2} = e * x_{2} = x_{2}. \] Therefore, \(x_{1} = x_{2}\), which is a contradiction.
      • Suppose \(\exists\ y_{1}, y_{2} \in G\) such that \(y_{1} * a = b\) and \(y_{2} * a = b\) and \(y_{1} \ne y_{2}\). \(\exists\ c \in G\) is an inverse of \(a\), there are \[ b * c = (y_{1} * a) * c = y_{1} * (a * c) = y_{1} * e = y_{1}, \] and \[ b * c = (y_{2} * a) * c = y_{2} * (a * c) = y_{2} * e = y_{2}. \] Therefore, \(y_{1} = y_{2}\), which is a contradiction.
      Hence, \(\exists !\ x, y \in G\) such that \(a * x = b\) and \(y * a = b\).
  • Unique inverses
    \(\forall\ a \in G\), because \(e \in G\), \(\exists !\ b \in G\) such that \(a * b = e\) and \(b * a = e\). Hence, \(b\) is the unique inverse of \(a\).

\(\blacksquare\)

Rules of combining powers in groups

\(\forall\) group \(\langle G, *\rangle\), \(\forall\ a \in G\),
  • \(\forall\ n \in \mathbb{Z}^{+}\) and \(a^{n*} = a^{*n}\).
    Both \(a^{n*}\) and \(a^{*n}\) are denoted as \(a^{n}\). The inverse of \(a^{n}\) is denoted as \(a^{-n}\). Let \(a^{0}\) denote the identity element of \(\langle G, *\rangle\).
  • \(\forall\ m, n \in \mathbb{Z}\), \(a^{m} * a^{n} = a^{m + n}\) and \(a^{m} * a^{n} = a^{n} * a^{m}\).
  • \(\forall\ m, n \in \mathbb{Z}\), \((a^{m})^{n} = a^{mn}\) and \((a^{m})^{n} = (a^{n})^{m}\).
Proof.
  • The proof is based on mathematical induction.
    1. For \(n = 1\), there is \(a = a^{1*} = a^{*1}\).
    2. For \(n = 2\), there is \(a^{2*} = a * a^{1*} = a * a = a^{*1} * a = a^{*2}\).
    3. \(\forall\ k \in \mathbb{Z}^{+}\) and \(k \geq 2\), for \(n = k\), suppose that there is \(a^{k*} = a^{*k}\).
    4. For \(n = k + 1\), there is \[ a^{(k + 1)*} = a * a^{k*} = a * a^{*k} = a * (a^{*(k - 1)} * a) = (a * a^{*(k - 1)}) * a = (a * a^{(k - 1)*}) * a = a^{k*} * a = a^{*k} * a = a^{*(k + 1)}. \]
    Hence, \(\forall\ n \in \mathbb{Z}^{+}\), \(a^{n*} = a^{*n}\).
  • \(\exists\ e \in \langle G, *\rangle\) is the identity element of \(\langle G, *\rangle\).
    • This proof is based on mathematical induction and proves the case \(\forall\ m, n \in \mathbb{Z}^{+}\),
      1. For \(n = 1\), there is \(a^{m} * a = a^{*m} * a = a^{*(m + 1)} = a^{m + 1}\).
      2. \(\forall\ k \in \mathbb{Z}^{+}\), for \(n = k\), suppose that there is \(a^{m} * a^{k} = a^{m + k}\).
      3. For \(n = k + 1\), there is \[ a^{m} * a^{k + 1} = a^{m} * (a^{k} * a) = (a^{m} * a^{k}) * a = a^{m + k} * a = a^{(m + k) + 1} = a^{m + (k + 1)}. \]
      Therefore, \(\forall\ m, n \in \mathbb{Z}^{+}\), \(a^{m} * a^{n} = a^{m + n}\).
    • This proof proves the case \(\forall\ m \in \mathbb{Z}\) and \(n = 0\). There is \[ a^{m} * a^{n} = a^{m} * a^{0} = a^{m} * e = a^{m} = a^{m + 0} = a^{m + n}. \]
    • This proof proves the case \(m = 0\) and \(\forall\ n \in \mathbb{Z}\). There is \[ a^{m} * a^{n} = a^{0} * a^{n} = e * a^{n} = a^{n} = a^{0 + n} = a^{m + n}. \]
    • This proof proves the case \(\forall\ m \in \mathbb{Z}^{-}\) and \(\forall\ n \in \mathbb{Z}^{+}\),
      • If \(m + n > 0\), because \(m + n > 0\) and \(-m > 0\), then there is \[ a^{-m} * a^{m + n} = a^{-m + (m + n)} = a^{n}. \] Therefore, \[ a^{m} * a^{n} = a^{m} * (a^{-m} * a^{m + n}) = (a^{m} * a^{-m}) * a^{m + n} = e * a^{m + n} = a^{m + n}. \]
      • If \(m + n = 0\), because \(m = -n\), then there is \[ a^{m} * a^{n} = a^{-n} * a^{n} = e = a^{0} = a^{m + n}. \]
      • If \(m + n < 0\), because \(-(m + n) > 0\) and \(n > 0\), then there is \[ a^{n} * a^{-(m + n)} = a^{n -(m + n)} = a^{-m}. \] Then, \[ e = a^{m} * a^{-m} = a^{m} * \left(a^{n} * a^{-(m + n)}\right) = (a^{m} * a^{n}) * a^{-(m + n)}. \] Therefore, \(a^{m} * a^{n}\) is the inverse of \(a^{-(m + n)}\), which is \(a^{m + n}\).
    • This proof proves the case \(\forall\ m \in \mathbb{Z}^{+}\) and \(\forall\ n \in \mathbb{Z}^{-}\),
      • If \(m + n > 0\), because \(m + n > 0\) and \(-n > 0\), then there is \[ a^{m + n} * a^{-n} = a^{(m + n) - n} = a^{m}. \] Therefore, \[ a^{m} * a^{n} = (a^{m + n} * a^{-n}) * a^{n} = a^{m + n} * (a^{-n} * a^{n}) = a^{m + n} * e = a^{m + n}. \]
      • If \(m + n = 0\), because \(m = -n\), then there is \[ a^{m} * a^{n} = a^{-n} * a^{n} = e = a^{0} = a^{m + n}. \]
      • If \(m + n < 0\), because \(-(m + n) > 0\) and \(m > 0\), then there is \[ a^{-(m + n)} * a^{m} = a^{-(m + n) + m} = a^{-n}. \] Then, \[ e = a^{-n} * a^{n} = \left(a^{-(m + n)} * a^{m}\right) * a^{n} = a^{-(m + n)} * (a^{m} * a^{n}). \] Therefore, \(a^{m} * a^{n}\) is the inverse of \(a^{-(m + n)}\), which is \(a^{m + n}\).
    • This proof proves the case \(\forall\ m \in \mathbb{Z}^{-}\) and \(\forall\ n \in \mathbb{Z}^{-}\). Because \(-m > 0\) and \(-n > 0\), there is \[ a^{-n} * a^{-m} = a^{-n - m} = a^{-(n + m)} = a^{-(m + n)}. \] Then, \[ e = a^{m} * a^{-m} = (a^{m} * e) * a^{-m} = \left(a^{m} * (a^{n} * a^{-n})\right) * a^{-m} = \left((a^{m} * a^{n}) * a^{-n}\right) * a^{-m} = (a^{m} * a^{n}) * (a^{-n} * a^{-m}) = (a^{m} * a^{n}) * a^{-(m + n)}. \] Therefore, \(a^{m} * a^{n}\) is the inverse of \(a^{-(m + n)}\), which is \(a^{m + n}\).
    To sum up, \(\forall\ m, n \in \mathbb{Z}\), \(a^{m} * a^{n} = a^{m + n}\). Then \[ a^{m} * a^{n} = a^{m + n} = a^{n + m} = a^{n} * a^{m}. \] Hence, \(\forall\ n \in \mathbb{Z}^{+}\), \(a^{m} * a^{n} = a^{n} * a^{m}\).
  • \(\exists\ e \in \langle G, *\rangle\) is the identity element of \(\langle G, *\rangle\).
    • This proof is based on mathematical induction and proves the case \(\forall\ m \in \mathbb{Z}\) and \(\forall\ n \in \mathbb{Z}^{+}\),
      1. For \(n = 1\), there is \((a^{m})^{1} = a^{m} = a^{m \cdot 1}\).
      2. \(\forall\ k \in \mathbb{Z}^{+}\), for \(n = k\), suppose that there is \((a^{m})^{k} = a^{mk}\).
      3. For \(n = k + 1\), there is \[ (a^{m})^{k + 1} = (a^{m})^{k} * a^{m} = (a^{mk}) * a^{m} = a^{mk + m} = a^{m(k + 1)}. \]
    • This proof is based on mathematical induction and proves the case \(\forall\ m \in \mathbb{Z}\) and \(\forall\ n = 0\). There is \[ (a^{m})^{n} = (a^{m})^{0} = e = a^{0} = a^{m \cdot 0} = a^{mn}. \]
    • This proof proves the case \(\forall\ m \in \mathbb{Z}\) and \(\forall\ n \in \mathbb{Z}^{-}\). Because \(-n > 0\), there is \[ (a^{m})^{-n} = a^{m(-n)} = a^{-mn}. \] Then, \((a^{m})^{-n}\) is the inverse of \(a^{mn}\). In addition, \((a^{m})^{-n}\) is also the inverse of \((a^{m})^{n}\). Therefore, \((a^{m})^{n} = (a^{m})^{-n}\).
    To sum up, \(\forall\ m, n \in \mathbb{Z}\), \((a^{m})^{n} = a^{mn}\). Then \[ (a^{m})^{n} = a^{mn} = a^{nm} = (a^{n})^{m}. \] Hence, \(\forall\ m, n \in \mathbb{Z}\), \((a^{m})^{n} = (a^{n})^{m}\).

\(\blacksquare\)

Inverse of the operation

\(\forall\) group \(\langle G, *\rangle\), \(\forall\ a, b \in G\), \((a * b)^{-1} = b^{-1} * a^{-1}\).
Proof. \(\forall\) group \(\langle G, *\rangle\), let \(e \in G\) be the identity element of \(\langle G, *\rangle\). There are \[ (a * b) * (b^{-1} * a^{-1}) = ((a * b) * b^{-1}) * a^{-1} = (a * (b * b^{-1})) * a^{-1} = (a * e) * a^{-1} = a * a^{-1} = e, \] and \[ (b^{-1} * a^{-1}) * (a * b) = ((b^{-1} * a^{-1}) * a) * b = (b^{-1} * (a^{-1} * a)) * b = (b^{-1} * e) * a = b^{-1} * b = e. \] Hence, \((a * b)^{-1} = b^{-1} * a^{-1}\).

\(\blacksquare\)

Definition of subgroups

\(\forall\) group \(\langle G, *\rangle\), \(\forall\ H \subseteq G\) and \(H\) is closed under \(*\), \(\langle H, *\rangle\) is a subgroup of \(\langle G, *\rangle\) iff \(\langle H, *\rangle\) is a group.
  • \(\langle G, *\rangle\) is the improper subgroup of \(\langle G, *\rangle\).
  • All other subgroups are proper subgroups of \(\langle G, *\rangle\).
  • \(\langle \{e\}, *\rangle\) is the trivial subgroup of \(\langle G, *\rangle\).
  • All other subgroups are nontrivial subgroups of \(\langle G, *\rangle\).

Conditions of subgroups

\(\forall\) group \(\langle G, *\rangle\), \(\forall\ H \subseteq G\), \(\langle H, *\rangle\) is a subgroup of \(\langle G, *\rangle\) iff
  • \(H\) is closed under \(*\).
  • \(e \in H\).
  • \(\forall\ a \in H\), \(a^{-1}\) is in \(H\).

Smallest subgroup containing one element

\(\forall\) group \(\langle G, *\rangle\) and \(\forall\ a \in G\), let \(H = \{a^{n}\ |\ n \in \mathbb{Z}\}\). \(\langle H, *\rangle\) is the smallest subgroup of \(\langle G, *\rangle\) containing \(a\).
Proof.
  • \(\langle H, *\rangle\) is a subgroup of \(\langle G, *\rangle\).
    • \(H\) is closed under \(*\).
      \(\forall a^{n_{1}}, a^{n_{2}} \in H\), where \(n_{1}, n_{2} \in \mathbb{Z}\), there is \(a^{n_{1}} * a^{n_{2}} = a^{n_{1} + n_{2}}\). Because \(n_{1} + n_{2} \in \mathbb{Z}\), \(a^{n_{1} + n_{2}} \in H\), so \(H\) is closed under \(*\).
    • \(e \in H\).
      Because \(0 \in \mathbb{Z}\), \(a^{0} \in H\), and \(a^{0} = e\), so \(e \in H\).
    • \(\forall\ a \in H\), \(a^{-1}\) is in \(H\).
      \(\forall\ a^{n} \in H\), where \(n \in \mathbb{Z}\), because \(-n \in \mathbb{Z}\), \(a^{-n} \in H\). Then \[ a^{n} * a^{-n} = a^{n + (-n)} = a^{0} = e. \] \(a^{-n}\) is the inverse of \(a^{n}\). Therefore, \(\forall\ a \in H\), \(a^{-1}\) is in \(H\).
  • \(\langle H, *\rangle\) is the smallest subgroup of \(\langle G, *\rangle\) containing \(a\).
    \(\forall\) subgroup \(\langle S, *\rangle\) of \(\langle G, *\rangle\) and \(a \in S\). Suppose \(|S| < |H|\). Then \(\exists\ a^{n} \in H\) such that \(a^{n} \notin S\), where \(n \in \mathbb{Z}\). \(\forall\ a^{i} \in S\), where \(i \in \mathbb{Z}\), there is \[ a^{i + 1} = a * a^{i} \in S. \] Therefore, \(a^{n} \in S\), which is a contradiction. Hence, \(\langle H, *\rangle\) is the smallest subgroup of \(\langle G, *\rangle\) containing \(a\).

\(\blacksquare\)

Definition of cyclic subgroups

\(\forall\) group \(\langle G, *\rangle\) and \(\forall\ a \in G\), \(\langle a, *\rangle = \{a^{n}\ |\ n \in \mathbb{Z}\}\) is called the cyclic subgroup of \(\langle G, *\rangle\) generated by \(a\).

Definition of cyclic groups

\(\forall\) group \(\langle G, *\rangle\) and \(\forall\ a \in G\), if \(\langle a, *\rangle = \langle G, *\rangle\), then \(\langle G, *\rangle\) is cyclic. \(a\) is called the generator of \(\langle G, *\rangle\), and we say that \(a\) generates \(\langle G, *\rangle\).
