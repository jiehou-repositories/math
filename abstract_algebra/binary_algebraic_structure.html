Binary Algebraic Structures

Definition of binary algebraic structures

A binary algebraic structure \(\langle S, *\rangle\) is a set together with a binary operation \(*\) on \(S\).

Definition of left identity element and right identity element

\(\forall\) binary algebraic structure \(\langle S, *\rangle\),
  • \(e \in S\) is called a left identity element of \(S\) iff \(\forall\ a \in S\), \(e * a = a\).
  • \(e \in S\) is called a right identity element of \(S\) iff \(\forall\ a \in S\), \(a * e = a\).
  • \(e \in S\) is called an identity element of \(S\) iff \(\forall\ a \in S\), \(e * a = a * e = a\).

Uniqueness of identity element

\(\forall\) binary algebraic structure \(\langle S, *\rangle\) has at most one identity element.
Proof.
Suppose \(\exists\ \langle S, *\rangle\) has at least identity elements \(e_{1}\) and \(e_{2}\), and \(e_{1} \ne e_{2}\). Then
  • Because \(e_{1}\) is a identity element, there is \[ e_{1} * e_{2} = e_{2} * e_{1} = e_{2}. \]
  • Because \(e_{2}\) is a identity element, there is \[ e_{2} * e_{1} = e_{1} * e_{2} = e_{1}. \]
Therefore, \(e_{1} = e_{2}\), which is a contradiction. Hence, \(\forall\) binary algebraic structure \(\langle S, *\rangle\) has at most one identity element.

\(\blacksquare\)

Definition of left inverse and right inverse

  • \(\forall\) binary algebraic structure \(\langle S, *\rangle\) such that \(\exists\ e \in S\) is either a left identity element or a right identity element of \(\langle S, *\rangle\),
    • \(\forall\ a \in S\), \(b \in S\) is called a left inverse based on \(e\) of \(a\) iff \(b * a = e\).
    • \(\forall\ a \in S\), \(b \in S\) is called a right inverse based on \(e\) of \(a\) iff \(a * b = e\).
    • \(\langle S, *\rangle\) has left inverses based on \(e\) iff \(\forall\ a \in S\), \(\exists\ b \in S\) such that \(b * a = e\).
    • \(\langle S, *\rangle\) has right inverses based on \(e\) iff \(\forall\ a \in S\), \(\exists\ b \in S\) such that \(a * b = e\).
  • \(\forall\) binary algebraic structure \(\langle S, *\rangle\) such that \(\exists\ e \in S\) is the identity element of \(\langle S, *\rangle\),
    • \(\forall\ a \in S\), \(b \in S\) is called an inverse of \(a\) iff \(b * a = a * b = e\).
    • \(\langle S, *\rangle\) has inverses iff \(\forall\ a \in S\), \(\exists\ b \in S\) such that \(b * a = a * b = e\).
